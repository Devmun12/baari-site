बारी Baari is Hindi for “turn”. As in, whose turn is it this week?

Who’s taking Ma to her follow-up on Thursday?

In a sibling WhatsApp group, the plan is in there somewhere. Finding it, and agreeing who does it, is the hard part.

  • Plans get buried under good-morning forwards

    The one message that matters scrolls away under sunrise photos and festival wishes.

  • The sibling who lives closest becomes the default

    Nobody decides it. They just end up doing the bills, the visits and the follow-ups, every time.

  • Things slip. The guilt doesn’t.

    A bill goes unpaid or a check-up gets missed, and everyone feels it, near or far.

How Baari works

Baari keeps your parents’ care in one shared list, run from WhatsApp. Forward a message, a Hindi or English voice note or a bill photo, and Baari turns it into a task with a date and an owner. Reminders reach whoever said yes, and slipped tasks move to the next sibling, so the one living closest is not doing it all alone. Baari never gives medical advice. It keeps Ma and Papa’s needs from falling through the cracks.

What Baari will and won’t do

Baari helps your family organise care. It is not a doctor, and it is not an ambulance.

112

Baari is not an emergency service.In an emergency, call 112.

  • Never gives medical advice

    Baari organises care. It does not diagnose, recommend treatment or answer health questions itself.

  • Health questions go to their own doctor

    When a health question comes up, Baari turns it into an Ask the doctor task for your parent’s own doctor.

  • Your parent’s consent comes first

    Baari asks for your parent’s consent, and they can withdraw it at any time.

  • Only what you forward is used

    Baari works only with what you forward to it, and is built around India’s Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023.

Questions about Baari

Does my parent need to install an app?

No. Baari runs on WhatsApp, which your family already uses. Nobody needs to install anything new. If you turn on the optional daily check-in, your parent gets it there, in Hindi or English.

Does Baari give medical advice?

No, never. When a health question comes up, Baari turns it into an “Ask the doctor” task for your parent’s own doctor.

Baari is not an emergency service. In an emergency, call 112.

Who can see the share of care?

Only the main caregiver. The weekly share of care view stays private to them unless they choose to share it with the family.

What does it cost?

Baari is free during the pilot. After that, we plan a family plan of around ₹149 a month. This price is indicative and may change.
